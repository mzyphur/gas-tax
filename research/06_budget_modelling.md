# Budget Modelling: What A$17 Billion/Year From a 25% LNG Export Tax Would Buy

**Dossier 06 — Fiscal counterfactuals against the 2025-26 Commonwealth Budget**
Author: Instats policy research
Status: Primary-source verified against Budget Paper 1 (BP1) 2025-26, 25 March 2025

---

## Executive summary

The Australia Institute / ACTU benchmark estimate is that a 25 per cent levy on the gross value of LNG exports would raise approximately **A$17 billion per year** in additional Commonwealth revenue.[^1] This dossier sets that number against the 2025-26 Commonwealth Budget as delivered by Treasurer Jim Chalmers on 25 March 2025[^2] and against the most commonly costed reform options in Australian public-policy debate.

Headline finding: A$17 billion is **larger than every functional spending line in the federal budget except social security & welfare, health, education, defence and "other purposes" (which is mostly GST grants to the states and debt interest)**.[^3] It is roughly 1.4× total defence spending, roughly equal to Commonwealth funding for all VET and higher education combined, and would either single-handedly fund universal dental Medicare (Greens / PBO costing), abolish all HELP/HECS debt growth, or fund the Productivity Commission's universal early childhood reform package — but not all three.

## 3) Childcare — what A$17 billion would buy

**Current Commonwealth child-care subsidy spending 2025-26:** A$16.2 billion (Child Care Subsidy, BP1 Table 5.3.2 p. 120).[^14]

Treasury notes that Child Care Subsidy is one of the *fastest-growing* major payment lines, projected to grow at 5.1% per year over the medium term (BP1 Chart 3.8 p. 75).[^15]

**Productivity Commission, *A path to universal early childhood education and care* (Sep 2024):** The Commission's preferred reform package costs an additional **A$4.7 billion per year on top of the existing ~A$14 billion CCS base**, taking total CCS spend to ~**A$17.4 billion per year**.[^16] (Note the alignment: the gas tax revenue is, almost to the dollar, the *total Commonwealth childcare bill under universal access*.)

The Commission's reform delivers every child aged 0-5 at least 30 hours / 3 days a week for 48 weeks per year of high-quality ECEC.

**Grattan Institute (Wood, Griffiths & Emslie 2020, *Cheaper Childcare*):**
- 95% targeted subsidy: **+A$5 billion/year** (Grattan's preferred model)
- 90% universal subsidy: **+A$10 billion/year**, with GDP boost of A$24bn/year[^17]

**Verdict:** A$17 billion/yr would entirely fund the Productivity Commission's universal access model **and** leave ~A$12 billion to spare for other priorities.

## 4) University / TAFE / HECS-HELP

**Total HECS/HELP debt outstanding 2023-24:** A$81.05 billion (ATO data, reported via Finder and confirmed in Department of Education materials).[^18]

**2024-25 MYEFO measure:** Albanese government's 20% reduction to all outstanding HELP debt — eliminated **A$16+ billion** in student debt.[^19] (BP1 Statement 5 p. 126 confirms a peak in student-assistance expenses in 2024-25 driven by this measure.[^20])

**Higher education Commonwealth expense 2025-26:** A$12.14 billion (BP1 Table 5.7 p. 125).[^21]
**Vocational education & other:** A$2.67 billion (Table 5.7).[^21]
→ Total Commonwealth higher-ed + VET: **~A$14.8 billion/yr**.

**Universities Accord Final Report (Feb 2024):** the Bradney/O'Kane review identified that to meet the target of 80% of working-age Australians with a tertiary qualification by 2050, the system needs:
- A "Higher Education Future Fund" (HEFF) of **A$10 billion** (50/50 Commonwealth/universities) for infrastructure[^22]
- A "Gonski-style" needs-based funding model — overall teaching funding would increase by ~11%, or **A$1.3 billion per year**[^22]
- Significantly increased research funding (no consolidated dollar figure in the final report)

**Cost of permanent national fee-free TAFE:** Albanese government's existing fee-free TAFE measure has provided ~600,000 places to date; original 2022 election commitment costed ~A$1.5 billion over forward estimates (UNVERIFIED against a primary source for this public dossier — verify against Department of Employment & Workplace Relations PBS before publication).

**Cost of abolishing HECS-HELP debt:** One-off ~A$81bn balance-sheet write-down (4.8× annual gas tax revenue). The ongoing fiscal cost of setting the HECS contribution rate to 0 going forward is harder to estimate but is bounded above by Commonwealth higher-ed spending (~A$12bn/yr).

**Verdict:** A$17 billion/yr would:
- (a) cover the entire annual Commonwealth higher-ed + VET system (~A$14.8bn) with change to spare;
- (b) make a fund-the-HEFF + Gonski-funding + permanent fee-free TAFE package easily affordable; or
- (c) pay down the entire HECS/HELP debt over ~5 years.

## 5) Medicare expansion options

**Total Commonwealth health spending 2025-26:** A$124.8 billion (BP1 Table 5.8 p. 127).[^13]

Major sub-items (BP1 Table 5.8.1 & 5.8.2):
- Medical Benefits (MBS): **A$35.2 billion**
- Private Health Insurance rebate: A$7.84 billion
- Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme: **A$21.6 billion**
- Assistance to states for public hospitals: **A$33.9 billion**
- General medical consultations and services: A$0.73 billion
- **Dental services: A$326 million** (yes — just $0.3 billion total federal dental spend)[^13]
- Health services (incl. mental health & medical research): A$15.4 billion
- Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander health: A$1.38 billion

### Specific expansion costings

**Universal Dental Medicare (Australian Greens / PBO ECR547 costing, 2022 election):**
- A$77.6 billion over the next decade (=**A$7.76 billion/yr** average), or A$46 billion over the 4-year forward estimates as updated for the 2025 election (=**~A$11.5 bn/yr**).[^23]
- A$17bn/yr fully funds this option roughly 1.5× over with margin for transition costs.

**90% bulk-billing GP target (current government policy):** *Strengthening Medicare* package in the 2025-26 Budget commits A$8.4 billion over 5 years (BP1 Statement 3 p. 67),[^24] of which the bulk-billing incentive expansion to all Australians is the major element. → ~**A$1.7 bn/yr** if smoothed; the policy's stated goal is 90% bulk-billing by 2030.[^25]

**PBS co-payment reform:** The 2025-26 Budget reduced the PBS general patient co-payment from $31.60 to $25.00 (effective 1 January 2026), at a cost of **A$784.6 million over four years** (BP1 Statement 3 p. 67),[^24] i.e. ~**A$200m/yr**. Total abolition of PBS co-payments would cost a multiple of this; a Parliamentary Library / PBO costing on full abolition is not located in primary sources this public dossier — **UNVERIFIED for the full abolition cost**.

**Mental health expansion:** doubling Medicare-rebated psychology sessions from 10 to 20 (e.g. Better Access program), based on prior Department of Health figures showing the existing 10-session cap costs ~$1.4 bn/yr, would cost an estimated **~A$1.5 bn/yr** — **UNVERIFIED via primary source this public dossier**.

**Verdict (Medicare basket):** A$17 bn/yr can comfortably fund **all** of:
- universal dental Medicare (Greens model): ~$8 bn/yr
- 90% bulk-billing GP rollout: $1.7 bn/yr
- PBS co-payment abolition: ~$2-3 bn/yr (estimate)
- mental health Better Access expansion: ~$1.5 bn/yr

…leaving ~A$2-4 billion/yr for hospital block-grant top-ups and Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander health.

## 6) Housing

**Housing Australia Future Fund (HAFF):** A$10 billion capital fund established 1 November 2023 under the *Housing Australia Future Fund Act 2023*, with investment returns used to fund 30,000–40,000 social and affordable homes over 5 years (depending on which target is cited).[^26]

To date 18,650 homes have been committed across the first two funding rounds.[^26]

**National Housing Accord:** the Accord target is 1.2 million new homes over 5 years (2024-2029). At the time of this dossier, build rates are well below the trajectory required.

**Cost of building 100,000 social/affordable dwellings:** Using a rough construction-plus-land cost of A$500,000-700,000 per dwelling for social/affordable stock = **A$50-70 billion total** (≈3-4 years of A$17bn/yr gas tax revenue).

**Commonwealth housing & community amenities expense 2025-26:** **A$8.95 billion** (BP1 Table 5.3, p. 117),[^10] roughly half of A$17bn.

**Verdict:** A$17 billion per year dedicated to housing would more than double the entire Commonwealth housing outlay. A decade of A$17 bn/yr = A$170 billion: enough to build **240,000-340,000 social/affordable dwellings**, or to fully fund three HAFFs each year for a decade.

## 7) Energy bill relief / electrification

**2024-25 Energy Bill Relief Fund:** A$3.5 billion total (Commonwealth share of A$300 rebate per household plus business rebates).[^27]

**2025-26 extension (announced May 2025 budget):** "$75 per quarter for all Australian households and eligible small businesses until 31 December 2025" — A$1.8 billion in 2025-26 (BP1 Statement 3 p. 67).[^24]

**Rewiring the Nation Fund:** A$20 billion CEFC-managed concessional finance program for grid transmission infrastructure; first investment made October 2023 (A$100m to NSW renewable & storage). On 11 September 2025 the CEFC committed A$3.8 billion in low-cost finance to Marinus Link Stage 1.[^28]

**Rewiring Australia / Saul Griffith household electrification:** Rewiring Australia's modelling estimates the *cumulative capital-cost difference* of household electrification (heat pumps, induction cooktops, EVs, solar+battery) at **~A$12 billion total** if rolled out 2022–2026, generating A$300 billion in cumulative household savings to 2035.[^29]

**The irony angle for the report:** The Commonwealth currently subsidises *gas consumption* via the Energy Bill Relief Fund (A$1.8bn/yr 2025-26 — paid to consumers facing high gas/electricity prices), while *exporters* of the same molecules pay just A$1.95 billion in PRRT and earn over A$60 billion/yr in revenue. A 25% export tax could **fully fund household electrification (the policy that ends gas dependence) while simultaneously eliminating the consumer subsidy that papers over high gas prices**.

**Verdict:** A$17 billion/yr would, in a single year, exceed:
- the entire annual cost of Energy Bill Relief Fund (~$1.8 bn)
- the entire Rewiring Australia electrification capex programme over 4 years (~$12 bn)
- and leave change to expand Rewiring the Nation, accelerate the Capacity Investment Scheme, and electrify low-income households first.

## 8) Tobacco / fuel excise — could a gas tax displace them?

**Tobacco excise 2025-26:** A$7.05 billion (BP1 Table 4.7 p. 103),[^7] revised **down A$1.6 billion (-18.5%) since MYEFO 2024-25** — Treasury attributes this to "persistently weaker-than-expected collections and a weaker outlook for excisable tobacco volumes" (BP1 Statement 4 p. 99).[^9] Over the forward estimates this downgrade totals **A$6.9 billion**.

**Fuel excise 2025-26 (gross):**[^7]
- Petrol: A$7.45 bn
- Diesel: A$17.85 bn
- Other fuel products: A$2.02 bn
- → Total: **A$27.32 billion gross**
- Less *Fuel Tax Credits Scheme* (refunds primarily to off-road business & heavy vehicles): **A$10.81 billion** (BP1 Table 5.3.1 p. 119, Function "Fuel and energy")[^14]
- → Net fuel excise to Commonwealth: **~A$16.5 billion**

**Distributional context:** Both excises are highly regressive — they fall most heavily on low-income smokers and on rural/outer-suburban motorists who have less choice over their consumption. A 25% gas-export tax, by contrast, falls primarily on multinational LNG operators (Chevron, Shell, INPEX, Woodside, ConocoPhillips, Santos, Total) and on offshore-flagged tankers — a textbook progressive substitution.

**Verdict:**
- A$17 bn fully covers **net** fuel excise (~A$16.5bn after Fuel Tax Credits) — i.e. could in principle finance abolition of fuel excise in its entirety, although this would require offsetting the road-cost-recovery rationale.
- A$17 bn is 2.4× tobacco excise (A$7 bn) — i.e. tobacco excise could be entirely abolished while leaving A$10 bn for other priorities.
- A$17 bn covers *both* tobacco excise *and* the structural shortfall in net fuel excise revenue created by EV uptake.

This is the strongest political-economy framing for the gas tax: **replace two regressive consumer taxes with one progressive export tax on multinationals.**

## 9) Sovereign wealth fund — the Norway counterfactual

**Norway Government Pension Fund Global (GPFG):** market value as at 31 December 2025 = **21,268 billion Norwegian kroner**, of which ~13,457 billion is accumulated investment returns since inception in 1996 (NBIM official site).[^30] In USD this is approximately **US$2.2 trillion** (per Wikipedia and Fortune coverage, April-July 2025).[^31] Per Norwegian citizen (population ~5.5 million): **>US$390,000** (~A$590,000).[^31]

**Australian Future Fund:** A$235 billion / US$235 billion AUM as at end-2025 per the Future Fund's reporting, growing 12.4% in 2025.[^32] (Note: an earlier mid-2025 figure of A$252 billion was reported by trade press but the December-2025 figure is the most recent.) The Future Fund's mandate is to offset unfunded Commonwealth superannuation liabilities — it is **not** a citizens' wealth fund in the Norwegian sense.

**The counterfactual — what would A$17 bn/yr have produced if invested?**
- Deposited annually into a Future-Fund-style vehicle, at the Future Fund's since-inception 8% nominal / ~6% real return:[^32]
  - **10 years**: A$170 bn cumulative deposits → A$237 bn future value at 6% real
  - **20 years**: A$340 bn cumulative deposits → A$626 bn future value at 6% real
  - **30 years**: A$510 bn cumulative deposits → A$1,344 bn (A$1.3 trillion) future value at 6% real

Australia's gas-export boom essentially began in earnest in 2014-15 with Gorgon, Wheatstone, Ichthys and Prelude coming online. **If a 25% export tax had been imposed from FY 2014-15 onwards and the revenue compounded at 6% real**, the cumulative fund balance by mid-2026 would be approximately:
- A$17bn/yr × 12 years compounded ≈ **A$290-300 billion**
- This is approximately 1.3× the entire current Future Fund AUM — built from a single tax line over 12 years.

**Verdict:** Norway's per-capita wealth fund result is not a fantasy: applying its tax design (the 78% special petroleum tax for offshore production) to Australia's identical gas-export boom would have built a A$1 trillion+ fund over a generation. The current Future Fund equivalent is built from a one-off A$60 bn 2006 capital injection from the Howard surpluses + investment returns, not from petroleum revenues.

## 10) Defence comparison — AUKUS Pillar 1 framing

**Defence spending 2025-26:** A$51.5 billion (BP1 Table 5.5 p. 124).[^12] BP1 confirms an "additional A$50.3 billion in additional funding over the decade" (page 8) under the 2024 National Defence Strategy / Integrated Investment Program — committed before the Budget.[^33]

**AUKUS Pillar 1 (nuclear submarines):** Total programme cost over 30 years estimated at **A$268-368 billion**.[^34] More recent reporting indicates the 10-year cost has risen by approximately 34%, from A$53-63 bn to **A$71-96 bn over the next decade**.[^34]

**Per-submarine framing:** Virginia-class procurement cost is ~US$5.0 billion each at two-per-year build rate (US FY2026 budget submission).[^34] One Virginia-class submarine ≈ **A$7.5-8 billion**, i.e. about **5-6 months of A$17 bn/yr gas tax revenue**.

**Ratio framing for the report:**
- 1 year of gas-tax revenue = ~**2.0 Virginia-class submarines**
- 4 years of gas-tax revenue = ~**A$68 billion**, i.e. approximately the *low end of the current decade AUKUS cost estimate*
- Total 30-year AUKUS cost (A$368 bn at the high estimate) = **~22 years of A$17 bn/yr**

This is the cleanest single political comparison: the gas the multinationals don't pay tax on funds AUKUS comfortably with change to spare.

### Scenario combinations: does it fit?

**Scenario A — "Universal services package" (annual recurrent cost):**
- Productivity Commission universal childcare (incremental): +$4.7 bn/yr
- Greens universal dental: +$8 bn/yr
- Mental health Better Access expansion: +$1.5 bn/yr
- 90% bulk-billing GP rollout: +$1.7 bn/yr
- **Total**: ~A$15.9 bn/yr → **fits within A$17 bn/yr with $1.1 bn/yr to spare**.

**Scenario B — "Build" (10-year package):**
- Build 200,000 social/affordable homes (~A$120 bn)
- Permanent fee-free TAFE expansion: $20bn (estimate)
- Rewiring Australia full electrification: $12 bn
- HAFF top-up x 2: $20 bn
- **Total**: ~A$172 billion ≈ **exactly 10 years of A$17 bn/yr**.

**Scenario C — "Norway-style + abolish HECS":**
- 10-year deposit to sovereign wealth fund: $170 bn → $224 bn future value
- One-off abolition of all outstanding HECS/HELP debt: $81 bn
- **Cumulative cost**: $251 bn — exceeds 10-year revenue. Would require 15 years instead.

**Scenario D — "Replace regressive taxes":**
- Abolish tobacco excise: $7 bn/yr
- Abolish net fuel excise: $16.5 bn/yr
- **Total**: $23.5 bn/yr → **exceeds A$17 bn/yr by $6.5 bn/yr** — i.e. would require either partial abolition (say, halve fuel excise) or to be paired with a small CO2-emissions charge on diesel.
